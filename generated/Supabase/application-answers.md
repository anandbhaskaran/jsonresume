## Async / remote experience

Most of my career has been distributed. At LumApps I work with teammates across Europe and the US; at Beekeeper I led work across 3 teams spread across time zones; at Obermatt I directed a fully remote engineering team; at Anand Creations I run solo. I have also been on-call for global production systems, which is async decision-making with a deadline.

What works for me:

- **Write it down first, meet only if the doc is unclear.** Decisions live in issues, PRs, and short design docs, not in someone's memory. Someone in the next time zone gets the same context I have.
- **Bundle notifications.** I treat every ping as spending trust. Digest updates over streams. Clear "needs response by X" tags when I actually need one.
- **Async standups with what / why / blockers.** Progress is a link, not a paragraph.
- **Overlap windows for the hard stuff.** A one-hour zone-overlap for the calls that need real-time back-and-forth (design reviews, incident retros) beats trying to force async through them.

Challenges: async makes silent misalignment cheap. Two people can spend a week going in different directions because nobody said the thing out loud. I now over-index on writing the assumption down early ("I am going to do X unless someone says otherwise by Y") and inviting disagreement explicitly. The other one is rapport: it takes deliberate 1:1s and non-work channels to build the trust that a hallway conversation would build for free.
